# The hybrid step

Four persona drafts are not four things to choose between — they're material for one thing
you build by hand. Budget about 10 minutes.

## The rule

**tl;dr frame on top, drill-down beneath.**

1. Read all four drafts side by side.
2. Take the **frame** (the opening, the thing said in the first 1-2 sentences) from whichever
   draft states the point most plainly — usually the executive or PM draft.
3. Take the **drill-down** (the ordering of supporting detail beneath the frame) from
   whichever draft sequences the evidence best for a reader who keeps going — usually the
   engineer or designer draft.
4. Merge: frame on top, drill-down beneath it, in that order. Cut anything the frame already
   said. Check every `[dropped: ...]` line across all four drafts — a claim dropped for one
   audience's word budget may belong in the hybrid for a general reader.
5. Read it once more for length. Cut, don't compress-and-hope.

The skill being demonstrated is the choosing, not the drafting — the four drafts are cheap;
picking the hybrid is where your judgment goes.

## Hybrid log template (6 lines)

Fill this in as you do the merge — it's short enough to read on a slide, and it's the part
worth keeping:

```
Frame taken from:       <persona>  — <one clause: why this framing won>
Drill-down taken from:  <persona>  — <one clause: why this ordering won>
Cut:                    <one clause: what got cut and why>
Restored from [dropped]: <claim, or "none">
Time spent:             <minutes>
Net result:             <one line: what changed reader's mind / what it's for now>
```
